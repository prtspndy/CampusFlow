# Student Organization System

*Build one system to run events, members and money in one place.*

## 1. The Challenge

Your task is to build one unified software platform that solves the real, day-to-day problems of running a student organization. Think of it as the digital backbone of a busy campus club. Read the scenes that follow as things that actually happen during a normal semester, and design the system that makes each of them easy.

## 2. Meet the Skyline Student Association

The Skyline Student Association runs events, sells memberships and merchandise, and manages a small budget raised from dues, ticket sales and fundraisers. New students join every month, volunteers plan events on the side, and the leadership team is elected every year. Everyone involved is a student with classes to attend, so nothing about running the club should take more time than it has to.

### How Things Run Today

- **Member sign-ups:** Tracked in a spreadsheet
- **Event tickets:** Sold at the door for cash
- **Club budget:** Tracked in a notebook
- **Announcements:** Sent one by one on WhatsApp
- **Volunteer expenses:** Reimbursed after chasing receipts

Members lose track of events, money goes missing, and nobody knows who still owes what.

## 3. A Semester with the Club

The scenes below follow one semester with the Skyline Student Association, from a new student signing up to the treasurer closing the books. Every one of them is a problem someone in the club faces today.

### A new student joins

Someone signs up at a table on campus. The club needs to know who they are, whether they have paid their dues, and what that membership gets them, like discounts on tickets and merchandise.

Memberships run out at the end of the year, and someone should be reminded to renew before it lapses. Whenever a member turns up at an event, it should be easy to check that they are, in fact, a member.

### Selling tickets for the spring gala

The club is hosting its biggest event of the year. Tickets need to go on sale online, with different prices for members and non-members, and a limit on how many seats are left.

On the night, someone at the door should be able to check a ticket in seconds, without a printed list. After the event, the club wants to know how many people actually showed up, and how much the event made.

### Announcing the next meeting

The club needs to tell every member about a meeting, a deadline, or a change of plan. Right now, someone types the same message into several WhatsApp groups and hopes everyone sees it.

A simple website and a mailing list would let the club post one announcement that reaches everyone, and keep a record of what was said and when, instead of it getting lost in a chat.

### Ordering hoodies for the club

The club wants to sell branded hoodies and T-shirts to members. Someone needs to collect sizes, take orders and payment, and know how many of each size are actually left in stock.

Selling online would let members order from their phones, at any hour, without someone manually noting down every order on paper and chasing people for payment afterwards.

### Planning a fundraiser

A small team of volunteers is organizing a bake sale to raise money for the club. Tasks need to be split up: who is baking, who is buying supplies, who is managing the table on the day.

Someone should be able to see, at a glance, what still needs to be done, who is doing it, and whether the fundraiser is on track, instead of everything living in someone's head or a group chat.

### The treasurer, at the end of the semester

The treasurer needs to explain where the club's money went: dues collected, tickets sold, merchandise sold, and every expense a volunteer was reimbursed for.

Right now, that means going through a notebook and a pile of receipts. The club wants one place that shows what came in, what went out, and how much is left, so anyone on the team can understand it at a glance.
